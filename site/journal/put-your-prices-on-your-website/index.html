Put your prices on your website (yes, even you)

The most common reason service businesses hide their prices is fear of scaring people off. The people it scares off were never going to buy.

18 Sep 2026
Quincy Lou studio4 min read
Get a free review

Every week someone tells us their work is too custom to price. Then, ten minutes later, they describe the three packages that make up most of their invoices. Most businesses have a typical range, they just have not written it down.

What hiding prices actually does

A visitor who cannot find a price has two choices: ring and ask, or leave. The ones who ring are often the ones with the smallest budgets, because they have the least to lose. The ones who leave include the client you wanted, who assumed you were either too expensive or not serious.

You do not need a full price list

A starting price is enough. So is a range with a sentence about what moves it. ‘Most kitchen installations land between £9,000 and £16,000, depending on the units and the plumbing’ tells people everything they need to decide whether to call.

  • Publish a ‘from’ price for each main service
  • Explain the two or three things that change the price
  • Say what is not included, so nobody feels misled later

In our experience the enquiries that follow are fewer but much better, and the first call stops being a negotiation.
